# Labelling rules in force

The rules Anton has decided for labelling the golden set, each under a
plain name with its full description. This is the index: one place to see
what is in force. The exact wording the readers get is in the labelling
guide (experiments/2026-09-27-answer-key/key-guide.md); the day each rule
was decided, with Anton's words, is in [verdicts.md](verdicts.md); rules
not yet decided are in [rule-backlog.md](rule-backlog.md).

Some rules were known by a number or letter while they were being
discussed ("rule 3", "rule A"). Those were positions in a list, nothing
more. The old nickname is given with each rule, because the notes on the
answer-key cards still use it.

The classifier's questions do not carry these rules yet (v6 predates them).

## Rules for every question

### Label the article, not what Anton cares about
The nine answers describe what the article is, from its text alone. How
much Anton wants to hear about it (a promotion's word ranks high, a
pundit's low) is the decider's job, set in settings, and never bends a
label.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### Only the saved text counts
Judge by the text we saved, because that is all the classifier will ever
see. If the live page is a video and the saved text only describes what he
said, "he speaks" is no (#44). Page furniture (menus, "read also" links,
other headlines) is not the article.
*In the guide from the start; confirmed on #44, 2026-10-03.*

### Only what is new
All nine answers describe the article's one main claim. Something
mentioned on the way is background: a recap of his injuries, his last
result, a booking restated as the premise of a preview or a pick, a rumour
recalled in a closing line, anyone's guess at his timing. Background does
not set the fact and does not turn a yes/no question to yes. Decide from
the article alone: dates, tense, "after his June loss", where the line
sits.
*Examples: #856 (fractures recapped in a year-in-review: health no), #462
(Gaethje guesses he won't fight until next year: no fact), #1002
(Pantoja's message; the rematch rumour is a closing line: no fact).*
*Decided 2026-10-02. Was called "rule 3" (and absorbed "rule 5").*

### No fact means nothing else either
When the fact is "no fact", how firm is "none" and the three news
questions (result, next fight, health) are all no.
*Decided 2026-10-02, as part of "only what is new".*

### A fact stated inside the main claim belongs to it
Background is what the writer adds around the main claim. A hard fact
stated inside the main claim, by the person the article is built on, is
part of it. Topuria's essay is mainly a reflection, but he lists his
fractures in it: the fact is health (#723).
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### The three news questions: the main fact or a second fact, never a mention
"Reports his result / next fight / health" say whether that kind of news
is part of the main claim: as the main fact, or as a second fact stated
beside it (a fight report that also names his next opponent). They never
mean "is it mentioned". When the fact is result, next fight or health, its
question is always yes.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

## How central is he

### New, not long
Between "only mentioned" and "one of several", judge what is new about
him, not how long his part is. A whole paragraph retelling his last fight
and injuries is "only mentioned". He is "one of several" only when the
article adds something new about him while sharing the stage.
*Decided 2026-09-28. Was called "rule A".*

### Talking about someone else
When he talks about another fighter or the sport, and nothing in the
article comes back to him, he is "only mentioned", even at length (#1260,
Donchenko comparing two other fighters). When the story is something he
did or got wrong and the other fighter answers him, he is "one of several"
(#373).
*Decided 2026-10-03.*

### A callout is the other fighter's story
When another fighter's own news names him as the opponent he wants, the
article is that fighter's story. He is never "main subject" in it, although
a callout is, read literally, "something someone said about him". And a
callout alone does not make him "one of several" either: it says what the
other man wants and nothing new about him. So he is "only mentioned"
(#273, #843, #871, #921). The same quote written up as a story about his
own options is his story (#913, main subject).
*Decided 2026-10-03. A softer version, where real content about him beyond
the callout would make him "one of several", is parked: backlog 15.*

### A two-fighter preview is one of several
A preview or feature on a fight of his that covers both fighters is "one
of several", whichever fighter brought the article to us (#619).
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

## Whose words

### Booked or fought
A fighter is "opponent side" only when the article itself says a fight
between the two is booked (a date, an event, "will face") or has already
happened. A callout, a wish from either man, a rivalry, a rumour or "the
UFC has not confirmed" does not make him an opponent: he is "other
fighter's side", even when a rumour with a date exists and he is the one
answering it (#820).
*Decided 2026-10-02, confirmed 2026-10-03 over the looser "reported as
news". Was called "rule 6".*

### Another fighter's camp is "other fighter's side"
A fighter not tied to a fight with him, and that fighter's coach, manager
or team, are all "other fighter's side": champions of other divisions,
friends and teammates included.
*Decided 2026-09-28.*

### A fighter is a fighter, active or retired
A former fighter on a podcast or at the commentary desk is "other
fighter's side" (or "opponent side"), not media. The venue does not change
who he is, and the text rarely says who has retired (#879, #905). People
who never fought (Rogan, journalists, commentators) are media.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

## What new fact

### Next fight means a specific fight exists or changes
A fight is set, announced, offered, in talks, moved or cancelled, or
rumoured with substance. His own word that he has a fight counts, even
before the promotion confirms it and even if he does not name the
opponent. When he will be ready, that he is ready, that nothing is booked,
or opponents named only as options are not next-fight news.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### A rumour needs a who, a when or a where
A rumour about his next fight counts as next-fight news, at "rumour"
firmness, only when the article gives it substance: who said it, or a date
or event. It counts even when its subject denies it (#820, a December
date). "Amid rumours" framing with no who, when or where is a wish piece
(#871). And the rumour must be what the article is built on, not the
backdrop to someone's opinion (#862 yes; #901, #913 no): that is "only
what is new" applied here.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### A wish is no fact, whoever makes it
A callout, a pundit's pick, a demand, or "I'd love to fight him" is no
fact, including when his own manager asks for the fight (#129) and when he
himself calls someone out after a win (#851). Who wants it is carried by
"whose words" and "what the source does".
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### Status update, after the specific kinds
News of where he stands as a fighter when no specific fight exists or
changes: he is ready, will not fight before a date, a return window,
nothing booked, opponents named only as options, training camp, an
announced return to public life. Someone must state it as a fact, not
guess. Pick a specific kind first (result, next fight, fight week event,
health, career move, personal life); status update only when the main news
is none of those. "None of these" is the last resort, for a fact that is
not about where he stands as a fighter (#228). It has no yes/no question
of its own.
*Examples: Dana White "ready to fight, not on the schedule" (#998 and nine
copies), "returns to public view in September" (#572, #592).*
*Decided 2026-10-02, after blind readers on all 300.*

### Health wins over a return date
When someone named says how he is or how the recovery is going, however
briefly, the fact is health, even if the headline and most of the text are
about when he will be back. Three write-ups of one coach interview (#152,
#402, #412) get the same label. If the article only says when he will be
ready and describes no injury or recovery, it is a status update.
*Decided 2026-10-02, with doubt; the return date is then in no label.*

### Health follows who states it
"Reports his health" is yes when someone the article names (he himself,
his team, the promotion, a named outlet) states his condition as a
statement of its own. The writer's recap or inference ("he seems
recovered") and an older report recalled in one line are no (#627).
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### A post or letter takes the label of what it says
His video letter to his son is personal life, not "back in public" (#620,
#627, #651), whatever the headline calls it. A reflection with no fact in
it is no fact.
*Decided 2026-10-02.*

### Result only for an account of the fight itself
The fact is result, and "reports his result" is yes, only for a fight
report or his bout on a results page. A statement, interview or callout
after a fight is no, however recent the fight and however much of it is
retold, even when the first sentence restates the win before the interview
begins (#1172, #851, #778).
*Decided 2026-10-03.*

### An interview is a place, not a fact
An interview, press conference or media day is where things are said. What
is said there takes its own value, or no fact. "Fight week event" is only
the routine of a fight: weigh-in, face-off, open workout, card order,
start times.
*In the approved axes, 2026-09-27.*

## How firm

### His own unsigned fight is "reported"
When he says he has a fight but nothing is signed or announced, how firm
is "reported": stronger than a rumour passed on by someone else, weaker
than official. Otherwise what he or the promotion states is "official or
done".
*Decided 2026-10-02.*
