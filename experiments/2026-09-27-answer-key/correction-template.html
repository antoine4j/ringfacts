Who the readers are

Every reader is a Fable agent that saw the labelling guide and the article text, never the answers. A card or a note names them like this:

  • First readers (27 Sept to 1 Oct): three readers, all 300 articles, all nine questions, under the guide as it was before almost every rule. Their majority answer is what a card shows before any correction. "First readers differed" means the three did not all agree.
  • Overnight reader (2 Oct): one more reader, only on answers where the classifier disagreed with the first readers.
  • 'New, not long' re-readers (2 Oct): two readers, the question "how central" only, on the 59 articles that rule could move.
  • Status-update pilot readers (2 Oct): two readers, three questions (what fact, how firm, next fight), testing the new value "status update". Notes that say "pilot" mean these.
  • Rule re-read (3 Oct): two readers, 35 articles, five questions, testing the rules decided that day.
  • Full re-read, readers A and B (3 Oct): two readers, all 300 articles, all nine questions, under the guide with all 27 rules. This is the reading that counts now. Each read 15 articles at a sitting.
  • Reader C (3 Oct): a third reading of the 26 articles where A and B differed, each read alone by its own agent.
How this page works, and where to start

Every article is on one side of the split (the chip on each card). Tune (155): the classifier questions are changed while looking at these. Check (40): used to test a change, never looked at while changing. Test (105): scored once, at the end. Correct any of them the same way: they are all the golden set; the side only decides what the classifier may be tuned on.

How an answer was reached: two Fable readers labelled each article separately and blind. "Both readers" means they agreed. Where they differed, an Opus reader (a different Claude model) answered that question too: "2 of 3 readers" means Opus sided with one of the two. "Readers split" means all three gave different answers, so there is no answer until you choose one.

"Classifier v6" beside an answer means the classifier disagrees with the readers there: one of them is wrong, so those are the answers worth reading.

What to check on each article: only the highlighted answers; each says why it is flagged (see the three labels above). The line above the answers names them. The rest are unanimous and shown faded; a glance is enough.

You don't need to read all 300. Work down the numbered filters:

  1. Where the readers differed: settle these first. They are grouped by the boundary the readers split on (next fight news or background, opponent or another fighter's side, how central, …), each group headed by a brief: the ways to read the question, the signals in the text, and two or three candidate rules with what each would decide. Decide the rule once; the articles under it follow.
  2. Where only the classifier differs: read just the flagged answers.
  3. Where the readers and the classifier all agree: check the 15-article sample. If it is clean, the rest of that group can stand.
