The idea in four steps

  1. Does the main news fit a specific kind? Result, next fight, fight week event, health, career move, personal life. If yes, use it.
  2. If not, is it news of where he stands as a fighter? Ready, not ready before a date, nothing booked, opponents only as options, training camp, back in public. That is the new value, status_update.
  3. Is it a new fact of some other kind? Not one of the six, and not about where he stands as a fighter (a past negotiation revealed, #228). That is none_of_these, the last resort.
  4. If there is no new fact at all (a wish, a callout, a pick, a guess), it is no_fact.

The line between next fight and status update: does a specific fight exist or change? If yes, next fight. If the article only says what he is doing or when he is available, status update.

Checked against the classifier vendor's writing rules

I re-read TypeSafe's pages on 2 October (questions, choice, score, yes/no, and the known weak spots of the model) and changed the classifier column where the first draft broke them. The readers' column is free of these limits, because the readers are full models that read the whole guide.

What the vendor saysWhat it means for this draft
"Write descriptions that separate the options from each other." Indirect instructions are answered less reliably."Pick the specific kind first, status update only if the others do not fit" is an indirect, two-step instruction, so I removed it from the classifier. The fence is carried by the "not for" lines of status update itself, which is the vendor's own method. The readers keep the order. Note: the answer still comes back as shares across all options that add up to 100%.
Negations, "only" and implied conditions are read at face value. Write directly.Status update was defined as "when no specific fight exists". It is now defined by what it is: readiness, availability, training. "Even unsigned and unnamed" moved out of the definitions and into an example.
One condition per yes/no question.The next-fight question is now one plain question. The list of cases sits in the yes and no definitions.
A scale measures one thing. Each level is judged without seeing its neighbours."How firm" already mixes who says it with how settled it is. An exception clause ("he says it, but unsigned") would make that worse, so the level summaries stay simple and the unsigned fight is added as a signal. This is the weakest part of the draft. If it misfires, the vendor's own pattern is a separate yes/no question ("is the fight signed or announced?") combined in code.
The model leans toward the option listed first.Status update goes late in the list, after personal life and before no fact. When we do run, one extra pass with the options reordered would show how much the order matters. That is a paid repeat (about a cent), so it needs your yes.
The question and its options must not ask for different things.The fact question said a fact is "something that happened or is set to happen". "He is ready" is neither, so I added one sentence saying a statement of his readiness, availability or training is a fact too.
Use "not for" and examples where neighbours overlap. Keep an "other" option.Already the shape of v6. Status update gets both. None of these stays.

2. Recovery and return date in one statement: health always wins

If someone named describes his condition, the fact is health, even when the same statement gives a return date. #152, #402 and #412 are three write-ups of the same interview with his coach, so they get the same label. 3. A wish is no fact, whoever makes it

Next fight stays strict: a specific fight exists or changes. His manager asking for a rematch (#129) is no fact. The labels "source: his manager" and "act: steers him" still carry it. Side effect: the "wish" level of how firm is no longer used by any article.
