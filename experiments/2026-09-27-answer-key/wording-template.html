Status update: the idea in four steps

  1. Does the main news fit a specific kind? Result, next fight, fight week event, health, career move, personal life. If yes, use it.
  2. If not, is it news of where he stands as a fighter? Ready, not ready before a date, nothing booked, opponents only as options, training camp, back in public. That is the new value, status_update.
  3. Is it a new fact of some other kind? Not one of the six, and not about where he stands as a fighter (a past negotiation revealed, #228). That is none_of_these, the last resort.
  4. If there is no new fact at all (a wish, a callout, a pick, a guess), it is no_fact.

The line between next fight and status update: does a specific fight exist or change? If yes, next fight. If the article only says what he is doing or when he is available, status update.

Three things you decided on 2 October

1. The name: status update

Broad, as you wanted, and it covers camp news. The "not for" lines do the fencing.

2. Recovery and return date in one statement: health always wins

If someone named describes his condition, the fact is health, even when the same statement gives a return date. #152, #402 and #412 are three write-ups of the same interview with his coach, so they get the same label. You were not fully sure of this one ("maybe I'm making this more complicated"). The cost is that the return date is not in the label.

3. A wish is no fact, whoever makes it

Next fight stays strict: a specific fight exists or changes. His manager asking for a rematch (#129) is no fact. The labels "source: his manager" and "act: steers him" still carry it. Side effect: the "wish" level of how firm is no longer used by any article.
