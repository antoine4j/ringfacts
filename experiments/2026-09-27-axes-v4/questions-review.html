Classifier v4 Questions

What the classifier will be asked about each of the 300 articles, word for word. New in v4, from JEV's documentation: three question types (choice, score, yes/no), options with "not for" and examples, the article sent as named fields (watched fighter, headline, outlet, date, text), and an in-list "none of these" in each choice question (the separate fit questions of v3 are gone: in isolation they saw only option names, not their definitions). Point at anything by number, for example "Q1 level 2". Nothing has been run yet.

Q1 centrality score · a position on ordered levels, low to high; every level gets a probability

How central is the watched fighter to this article? Judge by how much of the article is about him - his part of the main news the article is built on - not by whether the article carries news about him (other questions ask that) and not by what its first sentences say.

level 0He is not in the article's content at all.
  • his name appears only in links, navigation, a sidebar or 'read also'
level 1He is only mentioned.
  • his past fight retold as background in someone else's story
  • his name or one line in a list, card, ranking, results page or comparison
  • he talks about another fighter or the sport, not about himself, even at length
level 2He is one of several subjects.
  • a preview or feature on a fight of his covering both fighters
  • an article sharing its attention between him and one or two others
  • another fighter's own news that the article ties to a fight between the two of them
level 3He is the main subject.
  • the article is about something he did, or said about himself or his own career
  • the article is about something someone said, reported or predicted about him

Q2 source choice · picks one option; every option gets a probability

Whose words or act is the new thing this article carries about the watched fighter? The person it ORIGINATED with - never the outlet, website or account that relayed it. If a journalist or podcaster passes on what someone else said, the source is that someone. If several, the one the article's main news is built on. 'His' means the WATCHED fighter's.

himselfThe watched fighter speaks, posts or writes.
his teamPeople who work on his fighting: his coaches, trainers, conditioning staff, sparring partners, physio, doctor.
Not for: his manager or agent
his managerHis manager, agent or business representative.
opponent sideA fighter he is booked against or publicly linked to a fight with, or that fighter's coach or manager.
Not for: a fighter who merely names him or calls him out without a fight between them being discussed
Examples: the champion he lost to, talking about their rematch; his next opponent's coach at the pre-fight press conference
promotionThe promotion and its officials (the UFC, its president, matchmakers, official channels), athletic commissions, official records.
other fighterAny other fighter not booked or linked with him, however famous, including champions of other divisions, friends and teammates.
mediaA journalist, pundit, podcaster or analyst in their own voice, or the outlet's own writer; also a leak or report with no named person behind it.
Examples: a podcaster saying the promotion is discussing his rematch; a columnist's view of his chances
fansFan reaction, social media crowds, comment sections.
no oneNobody's words are the news: an event is reported as fact and nobody is quoted or credited with announcing it.
Examples: a results page listing his win; a weigh-in report
none of theseNone of the options above describes this article; a different answer is needed.

Q3 act choice · picks one option; every option gets a probability

What does the source do regarding the watched fighter? Pick the one the article's main news is built on.

reports an eventNobody's words are the news: something happened - he fought, weighed in, faced off, was booked, a fight of his was announced or cancelled.
speaks of himselfHe himself talks about his own situation, plans, past, condition, fight or life.
Not for: a reply by him to an attack aimed at him (that is answers_for_him)
Examples: how he rates himself; his own forecast for his fight; what he wants next
assesses himSomeone other than him judges him: his level, chances, performance, condition, style, place in the division.
Not for: him judging himself
predicts his fightSomeone other than him gives a pick or forecast for a specific fight of his: who wins, how, in which round.
Not for: his own forecast
calls him outSomeone other than him asks to fight him, challenges him, or trash-talks him.
answers for himA reply to an attack, a callout or a claim aimed at him - made on his behalf by someone else, or by him.
steers himSomeone other than him says what he should do next: whom he should fight, whether a rematch should happen, when to return, which weight class - including demands made on his behalf.
Not for: what he himself says he wants next
gives news of himSomeone other than him gives a factual update about him: his condition, recovery, training, whereabouts, mood or plans.
Not for: a judgement of how good he is (that is assesses_him)
none of theseNone of the options above describes this article; a different answer is needed.

Q4 fact choice · picks one option; every option gets a probability

What new FACT about the watched fighter does the article carry, if any? A fact is something that happened or is set to happen, not an opinion about him. Choose by the article's main news. An interview, a press conference or a media day is a place where things are said, not a fact: if something said there is the news, choose that fact, or none if it is only talk.

bookingA fight of his being set, offered, negotiated, discussed as likely, or cancelled.
resultThe outcome of a fight of his.
fight week eventThe routine of a fight of his, and nothing more: a weigh-in, a face-off, an open workout, the card order, start times, how to watch.
healthAn injury, a medical issue, surgery or recovery.
return dateWhen he will fight again or be available: a return date or a timeline.
career moveRetirement, a contract, a move of weight class or team, a title vacated, stripped or awarded.
personal lifeHis life outside the cage: family, childhood, home, hobbies, money, a life lesson.
no factOnly opinion or talk: no new fact about him.
none of theseNone of the options above describes this article; a different answer is needed.

Q5 firmness score · a position on ordered levels, low to high; every level gets a probability

How established is the main fact this article asserts about the watched fighter, whatever kind of fact it is?

level 0No fact about him is asserted: only opinion, analysis or talk.
level 1A wish or a demand: someone wants it or says it should happen.
  • a fighter asking for the fight
  • his camp demanding a rematch
level 2A rumour: unnamed sources, a leak, 'talks', 'discussions', 'plans'.
  • a podcaster says the promotion is discussing it
  • 'sources say'
level 3Reported: a named outlet or journalist states it, citing sources.
  • 'ESPN reports that...'
level 4Official or done: the promotion or he himself states it, or it has happened.
  • the promotion announces the bout
  • he posts that he signed
  • a fight result
