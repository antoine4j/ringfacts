The Claims Layer

Where the unit of news stops being the article and becomes the fact. A design for claim extraction, corroboration, and the rumor → confirmed lifecycle. No code — architecture only. 2026-08-06; amended 2026-08-07 with first live-data findings.

1Why articles stop being enough

Everything built so far treats the article as the atom of news: fetch articles, dedup articles, post articles. That model has already shown its two failure modes in production:

  • Retellings slip through. Three articles covered one Masvidal quote about Fighter B (MMA Junkie original → XSPORT translation → heavy.com retelling). Headline similarity linked two of the three; the third posted as "new news." The group heard the same quote twice. (Update 2026-08-08: the cluster has grown to seven sightings across six outlets and two languages — and a fourth retelling slipped the embedding gate at 0.77 similarity. The problem compounds daily.)
  • Corroboration is invisible. When a second independent outlet reports the same fight, that is the most important signal in the whole system — a rumor hardening into fact. Today it is (at best) silently deduplicated. The signal is literally discarded.

Both problems have the same root: the thing the group cares about is not an article. It's the underlying fact — "Fighter B fights Alvarez at UFC 328 on May 9." Articles are just sightings of that fact. Step 5 makes the fact itself a first-class database object: a claim.

2The two-layer model

The architecture splits into two worlds. The red corner is the evidence world — raw articles, exactly what the hunter collects today. The blue corner is the fact world — canonical claims distilled from that evidence. Articles are immutable observations; claims are living records with a status that changes as evidence accumulates.

Fig. 1 — Three sightings, one fact. The Masvidal cluster as it should have been stored.

Items — the evidence

  • One row per article sighted, forever
  • Immutable: never edited after insert
  • Carries: URL, headline, body, embeddings, dedup audit trail
  • Exists today (built in 2b/2c/2d/2e)

Claims — the facts

  • One row per real-world assertion
  • Living: status evolves with evidence
  • Carries: canonical English text, typed fields, status, source links
  • New in step 5
Canonical English. Claims are always written in English regardless of source language (per spec §17.5's storage design). The Ukrainian article and the American article map to the same English claim text — which is also what makes claim embeddings comparable across the whole archive. Output language for the group is a separate presentation decision.

3Data model

Three tables. items already exists — it gains nothing new in step 5 (its held-audit columns from 2d stay purely about article-level dedup). The two new tables carry the fact world:

Fig. 2 — The schema. Red = exists, blue = new. Arrows are foreign keys.
How to read the arrows. An arrow like item_id → items.id is a foreign key: the column holds another table's row ID — a pointer. And claim_sources is a join table: each of its rows stores not a thing but a relationship — one row reads as the sentence "article #12 is evidence for claim #41, as an echo, asserting it, linked Tuesday." item_id says which article, claim_id says which claim, and the remaining columns (role, stance, linked_at) describe the link itself. Why a separate table instead of a claim_id column on articles? Because the relationship is many-to-many — one claim collects many articles (the Masvidal quote has six), and one article can support several claims (an announcement piece that also mentions an injury) — and because the link carries its own facts, it needs its own rows. The n:1 labels say "many link-rows can point at one article / one claim."

Notes on the interesting columns:

  • facts jsonb — the structured extraction: {opponent, event, date, location, promotion} for an announcement; {method, round} for a result. Typed enough to query ("all upcoming fights"), flexible enough to differ per claim type.
  • embedding — the canonical English text gets its own vector, so incoming articles are matched against claims the same cheap way articles are matched against articles.
  • tg_message_id — the Telegram message the bot posted for this claim. This is what lets the bot later reply to its own rumor post with "✅ now confirmed" — threading the story.
  • supersedes — claims are never edited when reality changes; a new claim supersedes the old ("fight moved to June 6" supersedes "fight on May 9"). History stays intact.

4The pipeline, end to end

The claims layer bolts onto the back of the existing hunter run. Everything up to the dedup ladder is unchanged. The key subtlety: held duplicates don't exit the pipeline anymore — an article held as a duplicate is exactly a "same claim, another source" event, which is evidence the status engine wants.

5Claim matching in detail

This is the same escalation ladder used everywhere in the system, aimed at a new question. Instead of "have I seen this article?", the matcher asks: "which fact is this article about?"

  1. Geometry orders, but does not cut. The article's embedding is compared against claim embeddings — and all active claims for that fighter (a naturally small set, ~10) go to the LLM, similarity-ordered. Amended 2026-08-07: a top-3 cutoff was measured risky — metaphor headlines ("Makhachev's heir will make Ilia throw in the towel") can bury the right candidate, whose entity hides behind a flourish only the LLM can resolve.
  2. The LLM decides. One call, given the article's headline + body excerpt and the candidate claims' canonical texts. It returns a verdict:
Fig. 4 — One matcher call, three possible verdicts. UNRELATED with an empty candidate list short-circuits to NEW.

Details that matter:

  • The extraction rides in the same call. When the verdict is NEW, the same response carries the canonical English text, the claim type, the structured facts, and a sourcing signal read from the article's own language — "officially announced" reads different from "sources say" and "in talks", and that phrasing is what seeds the initial status (§6).
  • One article can assert several claims. A fight-announcement article often also contains an injury note or a quote. V1 keeps it simple: extract the single primary claim per article; multi-claim extraction is a later refinement (§11).
  • Held duplicates skip extraction. An item held against another article inherits that article's claim link as an echo — no LLM needed. Only the judge-gray or genuinely-new items pay for a matcher call.
  • NO_CLAIM is a legal verdict (amended 2026-08-07). Some articles assert nothing about fights at all — the Spanish press delivered Fighter C's favorite restaurant and his Ibiza vacation on day one. Forcing extraction there manufactures garbage; instead the article posts through the normal digest with no claim created, and the type vocabulary gains a lifestyle category for the presentation tiers.
  • Identity check is part of the matcher contract (amended 2026-08-08). Name match ≠ fighter match: the live archive already caught a namesake esports driver ("Yaroslav Fighter B" in an iRacing video), Ilia's brother Fighter C's brother (also UFC), and keyword-stuffed junk with no connection at all. Before returning MATCH or NEW, the matcher must confirm the article concerns the watched fighter, the human — wrong-subject items resolve to a WRONG_SUBJECT outcome (recorded, never posted, never a claim). This absorbs spec step 3's "fighter filter" into the matcher.

6The lifecycle: rumor to confirmed

Statuses live on the claim, never on articles. Transitions are driven by a rules engine, not an LLM — the LLM's job ends at "what does this article assert, and how is it sourced?" Deterministic rules then count evidence. This keeps the most consequential behavior (telling the group something is confirmed) auditable and boring.

Fig. 5 — The state machine. Green edge is the one the group cares most about.
StatusMeaningEntry rule (deterministic)
Rumor One reporting chain asserts it; hedged sourcing New claim from a single non-official source, or extraction flagged "reported / rumored" language
Confirmed Fact, as far as the bot is concerned Official source (promotion site/account, per curated list) asserts it — instantly; or a second independent chain asserts it (§7)
Denied Credibly contradicted A source with stance denies attaches, and rules rank it (official denial > original outlet's walk-back > other)
Stale Rumor that never hardened No new evidence for 14 days (tunable); revives automatically if evidence arrives
Superseded Replaced by an updated fact New claim created with supersedes pointing here (date change, opponent change)

7Independence — the hard 20%

"Two sources" is worthless if the second one copied the first. XSPORT translating MMA Junkie is one reporting chain, not two — the whole corroboration logic hinges on telling echoes from independent reports. Signals, cheapest first:

  • Attribution text (strongest). Echoes usually confess in their own body: "as reported by MMA Junkie", "according to ESPN sources". The matcher LLM already reads the body — it returns an attributes_to field for free. If article B credits A's outlet → echo.
  • Original reporting markers. "told us", "in an interview with this publication", exclusive quotes → origin candidate.
  • Timing. Attachment hours after the origin with no own attribution → echo-leaning; the earliest sighting in the cluster is the presumed origin (works: MMA Junkie preceded both echoes).
  • Same-org detection. A curated map collapses outlet families (Yahoo Sports syndicating MMA Junkie's parent, etc.) — plain config, grown lazily as false corroborations appear.

Default when unsure: echo. The asymmetric cost rules this: a false "confirmed" tells the group something is fact when it isn't; a delayed confirmation just waits for the promotion's own announcement, which always comes. Bias toward skepticism.

8What the group actually sees

Posting stops being "one message per article" and becomes "one message per claim event." This composes directly with the step-4 reframe (classifier + presentation tiers): the claim type picks the ceremony, the claim event picks the timing.

Claim eventPost behaviorNoise level
New claim, born confirmed, type = announcement Ceremonial post 🚨 (own message, bold card, where-to-watch when step 6 lands), pinned, loud notificationLoud
New claim, rumor Quiet post, visibly tagged as rumor — the group loves gossip, it just needs honest labelingSilent
Rumor → confirmed Reply to the original rumor message (via stored tg_message_id): "✅ Confirmed — Bellator announced it." Threaded story.Loud
Corroboration attached (still rumor) No new message. Optionally edit the original post to append "also: Sherdog" (Telegram lets bots edit their own messages)None
Rumor → denied Reply to original: "❌ Fight is off per …"Normal
Superseding claim Reply to old claim's message + fresh post for the new fact ("📅 Moved to June 6")Normal
Non-lifecycle types (quotes, interviews, previews) Stay in today's hourly digest format, one line per claim (not per article — retellings collapse)Silent

The escalation-ladder economics hold: the archive can grow 100× and only the pgvector search notices; LLM spend tracks the news rate, not the archive. Fits inside the planned $10 Anthropic cap with a 10× margin.

10Failure modes & degradation

FailureBehaviorPrinciple
Matcher LLM down / errors Item is stored unclaimed and still posts via today's article-level rules. A reconciler pass (rerun of the matcher over unclaimed items — same idempotent shape as backfill-embeddings.js) attaches them later. Fail open; never lose news
Bad merge (two different fights matched into one claim) Worst realistic bug. Mitigations: candidate shortlist is same-fighter only; facts mismatch (different opponent/event) blocks MATCH in the prompt contract; unmerge = delete the claim_sources row, rerun matcher. Make wrong states cheap to repair
Wrong confirmation (echo miscounted as independent) Bias-to-echo default (§7) makes this rare; if it happens, status is recomputable — the rules engine can re-derive status from stored evidence at any time. Nothing is destroyed by a wrong transition. Status is derived state, always recomputable
No body available (Google-wrapped URL, paywall) Matcher runs on headline alone; attribution signals unavailable → source defaults to echo-leaning, claim still forms. Weakest evidence, weakest weight. Patchy evidence degrades weight, not function
Extraction hallucination (facts that aren't in the article) Canonical text must quote-anchor: the prompt contract requires the assertion to be supported by the provided excerpt; UNSURE-style abstention (no claim) is an allowed verdict, item posts normally. Abstention beats invention

11Open questions (decide at build time)

  • Matcher model: Haiku first (cost table assumes it); escalate to Sonnet only if extraction quality on real Ukrainian-language articles disappoints. Measure, then decide.
  • No Mastra for this layer (decided 2026-08-08): the claims layer is single-shot structured LLM calls + deterministic rules + SQL — none of the shapes (tool-using agents, durable workflows, cross-agent tracing) that justify an agent framework. Plain JS against the SDK. Mastra + TypeScript enter with the step-6 conversational responder, where their concepts are load-bearing; retrofitting the matcher under its tracing later is optional and cheap.
  • The matcher absorbs the 2d judge (realized 2026-08-08): "is this the same story?" (2d) is a special case of "which claim does this belong to?" (§5) — a MATCH verdict with role echo is the gray-zone dedup decision. If step 5 starts soon, the standalone judge slice is redundant work; the gray zone routes through the matcher instead.
  • Official-source list: which domains/accounts count as "official" per promotion (UFC, PFL/Bellator)? Small curated config; needs research per fighter's org.
  • Multi-claim articles: V1 extracts one primary claim per article. When does that start losing real news? (Watch for it in the unclaimed/UNSURE logs.)
  • Edit vs. reply for corroboration: silently editing old posts keeps the chat clean but rewrites history; replies are honest but noisier. Taste decision for Anton.
  • Staleness window: 14 days is a guess. MMA rumor cycles (negotiation → announcement) might argue for 30.
  • Cross-fighter claims: "Fighter B vs Fighter A announced" touches two watched fighters — one claim with two fighter links, or two claims? (Lean: one claim, fighter becomes an array or a join — decide when schema lands.)
  • Sequencing: claims land best after 2e (bodies make extraction and independence reliable) and can absorb step 4's classifier — claim type is the classification. Steps 4 and 5 may merge into one build arc.
  • Bootstrap pass (added 2026-08-07): the archive already contains ready-made claim clusters — the Masvidal quote (6 sightings, 2 languages) and the Fighter A UFC-Paris announcement (5 sightings). The reconciler's first job is a batch pass converting accumulated history into claims — same idempotent machinery as failure recovery, pointed backward. This is also the build's natural acceptance test.
  • Google's own clustering as a free signal (added 2026-08-07): the captured rss_description column carries Google News's related-coverage links per item — potentially a ready-made "these articles are the same story" hint to weigh alongside embeddings in the matcher. Mine before building anything clever.
  • Gray-zone volume measured (added 2026-08-08): ~10–13 items/day land in the 0.65–0.80 ambiguous band — real duplicates (the fourth Masvidal retelling at 0.77) mixed with genuinely-new developments (a manager feud producing distinct articles at 0.77–0.80). Confirms the 2d judge has daily work at ~1¢/day, and that the matcher volume estimate in §9 holds.
  • Source blocklist (added 2026-08-08): keyword-stuffing aggregators exist (two junk items from one source in one day). A small curated blocklist, grown lazily like the outlet-family map in §7, is cheaper than classifying garbage repeatedly.
  • Storyline threading (added 2026-08-08, parked): multi-day narrative arcs (prediction → rebuttal → counterattack) unfold as several related claims. The group's mental model may eventually want a "storyline" grouping above claims. Observed, not designed — revisit after claims ship.
