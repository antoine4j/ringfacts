Architecture Overview

1The system at a glance

FighterBot tracks a private watchlist of subjects — currently three MMA fighters, shown here as Fighter A, Fighter B, Fighter C — for a Ukrainian fan group on Telegram. There is deliberately no UI: the Telegram group is the entire product surface. One repo, one container image, two entry points: a webhook service (the future conversational rail) and the hourly hunter job — same image, different --command/--args.

The domain seam (2026-08-09). The pipeline itself no longer knows what a "fighter" is — the DB column, the watchlist field, and every internal name are subject (§3, renamed from fighter throughout the code and schema). MMA-specific knowledge — the outlet list, the official-source rule, the claim vocabulary, the prompt language — moved into one file, domain/mma.js, selected by the DOMAIN env var. A second, unrun domain (domain/example-music.js) exists purely as proof the seam holds for a different subject area, not as working configuration. The watchlist itself — who is actually tracked — lives in a gitignored watchlist.js and is never committed; every name in this document is a stand-in for that reason.

The design philosophy that runs through everything: escalate intelligence before information. Cheap checks (string compare) run before geometry (embeddings), geometry before a small LLM, and the LLM only sees items the free gates couldn't settle. Same ladder, every layer.

2The two-layer model

The core idea of the claims layer, live since 2026-08-08: the thing the group cares about is not an article — it's the underlying fact. The red corner is the evidence world: raw articles, immutable, exactly what the hunter collects. The blue corner is the fact world: canonical claims distilled from evidence, living records whose status changes as evidence accumulates.

The problem this solved in production: three-then-seven sightings of one Masvidal quote about Fighter B (MMA Junkie original → XSPORT translation → heavy.com retelling → …) — retellings kept slipping the article-level gates, and corroboration (the most important signal in news!) was being silently discarded as "duplicate."

Fig. 2 — Many sightings, one fact. The real Masvidal cluster: 5 linked sources in the live database.

Items — the evidence

  • One row per article sighted, forever
  • Immutable: never edited after insert
  • Carries: URL, headline, embedding, dedup audit trail
  • Live since 2b/2c

Claims — the facts

  • One row per real-world assertion
  • Living: status evolves with evidence
  • Carries: canonical English text, typed facts, status, own embedding
  • Live since step 5 phase 1 (2026-08-08)
Canonical English. Claims are always written in English regardless of source language. The Ukrainian article and the American article map to the same English claim text — which is also what makes claim embeddings comparable across the whole archive. Presentation language is a separate decision (§8).

3Data model

Three tables, all live in Neon. items carries the evidence plus a full dedup audit trail (why was this held, against what, at what similarity); claims and claim_sources carry the fact world.

Fig. 3 — The schema as deployed. Arrows are foreign keys.
How to read the arrows. An arrow like item_id → items.id is a foreign key: the column holds another table's row ID — a pointer. And claim_sources is a join table: each of its rows stores not a thing but a relationship — one row reads as the sentence "article #12 is evidence for claim #4, as an echo, asserting it, linked Tuesday." Why a separate table instead of a claim_id column on articles? Because the relationship is many-to-many — one claim collects many articles (the Masvidal quote has five), one article can support several claims — and because the link carries its own facts (role, stance, when), it needs its own rows.

The columns that make the system honest:

  • nearest_similarity / nearest_item / held_reason — the dedup audit trail. Every held item records what held it and how close it was; threshold tuning (0.85 → 0.80) came from reading these columns, not from guessing.
  • tg_message_id — the Telegram message the bot posted for a claim. This is what lets the bot later reply to its own rumor post with "✅ Confirmed" — threading the story.
  • supersedes — claims are never edited when reality changes; a new claim supersedes the old ("moved to June 6" supersedes "on May 9"). History stays intact. (Column exists; the flow that writes it is phase 2.)
  • rss_description — Google News's own related-coverage links per item. Captured "for later mining" on 2026-08-06; the mining arrived with 2e — it now feeds the matcher as secondary evidence for archive items with no body.
  • resolved_url / body / body_fetched_at (2e) — the real article address once Google's wrapper is decoded (same as url for direct-feed items), and the extracted article text (capped ~10k chars). Null body = headline-only; every consumer tolerates it. url stays the identity; both columns feed the URL gate so the same story arriving from two rails is one item.
  • body_via (2e) — which rung of the extraction ladder produced body, or which failure stopped it (feed-content is the richest rung; og-description the thinnest). Quality telemetry, not decoration: it is what let the 2026-08-10 dedup fix (§5) trace a wrong post back to the specific rung that produced the doubled body.
  • digest_tier (2026-08-09, lib/tier.js) — main or tangential, set only on items that actually posted. Decides whether an article earns its own headline or folds into a shared "↘ Also mentioning" line (§5).

4The hourly pipeline

Every hour at :17, the hunter sweeps a 24-hour window of two discovery rails: Google News RSS per fighter per language edition, and — since 2e — six direct publisher feeds (fetched once, filtered per fighter by name; real URLs, and an independent rail if Google sheds our IP). The window overlapping the hourly cadence ~24× is the reliability trick: any single failed run loses nothing, because the next run re-covers it. Each unseen item then descends the ladder — cheap gates first, and network only for items that earned it:

Fig. 4 — One hourly run, as deployed. Amber path: held duplicates don't exit — they become claim evidence. Green box: the 2e body step, network only for items past the free gates.

Details that matter in production:

  • The body step is downstream of the free gates on purpose (2e, spec §11 shallow-before-deep): held dups and known URLs never cost network. For each matcher-bound item — decode Google's wrapper (zero-network base64 for current tokens; the batchexecute fallback has a per-run circuit breaker), catch the cross-source duplicate a decode can reveal (same story already stored from a direct feed → held as echo through the drift guard), then fetch and extract the article (zero-dependency ladder: feed content → JSON-LD articleBody → <article> → paragraphs → og:description, the winning rung logged as quality telemetry). Direct-feed items usually skip the fetch entirely — WordPress feeds ship whole articles in content:encoded.
  • The flood cap applies to unseen items only (max 5 per fighter per run, newest-first, truncation logged). An earlier version capped before the URL gate — which let already-known items permanently shadow older unseen ones. Found by questioning, fixed.
  • Held ≥ 0.80 inherits the claim of its nearest neighbour as an echo — corroboration recorded for free, no LLM spent. The 0.80 threshold is measured, not guessed: a real translated pair scored 0.841, unrelated pairs ≤ 0.702.
  • …but inheritance is checked against the claim itself (added 2026-08-08). Inheritance is transitive — B is held against A and takes A's claim, then C is held against B and takes it too — and every hop only has to clear 0.80 against the previous headline, so a chain can walk somewhere its starting claim never was. It did: a story about Fighter C's manager blasting Ali Abdelaziz rode a 0.802 → 0.869 → 0.974 chain onto an unrelated matchmaking claim. Before linking, the item is now compared to the claim's own canonical text; if some other claim fits at least 0.10 better, the item stays held but unlinked (a wrong evidence row is worse than none — phase 2 counts these rows for corroboration, and a confirmation post lists them to the group). The 0.10 came off the live data: measured across all 28 links, drifted ones sat 0.107–0.214 below the item's best-fitting claim, while correct-but-awkward ones sat 0.076–0.082 below. Matcher-made links are never second-guessed — only the cheap gate is.
  • Everything is idempotent. Re-running any part (migrations, backfills, the hunt itself) is always safe — the URL gate and IF NOT EXISTS guards make reruns no-ops.

5Claim matching in detail

The matcher is the intelligence concentrated at the top of the ladder. For each item that survives the free gates, one Haiku call receives the headline, source, timing, a body excerpt when one exists (first 1200 chars — 2e; archive items get Google's related-coverage cluster from rss_description instead) — and all active claims for that fighter, similarity-ordered ("order, don't cut": a top-3 cutoff was measured risky because metaphor headlines bury the right candidate). Forced tool use means the answer is always a parseable verdict — no JSON scraping. Five verdicts:

VerdictMeaningWhat happens
MATCHSame underlying fact as a listed claim (retelling, translation, syndication) Linked via claim_sources with stance asserts/denies; role official if the source is ufc.com, else echo. Official + asserts → confirms the claim (§6). No new post.
NEWA claim-worthy fact not in the list Claim inserted (type, canonical English sentence, structured facts, sourcing signal); canonical text embedded for future matching; posts per §8.
NO_CLAIMAbout the fighter, but asserts nothing claim-worthy — lifestyle, peripheral division mentions, reactions Article posts through the normal digest; no claim created. The group still gets its gossip.
WRONG_SUBJECTNot about this fighter at all: namesakes (an iRacing driver also named Fighter B), relatives (Fighter C's brother), keyword-stuffed junk Recorded with held_reason='wrong_subject', never posted, never a claim. This absorbed the planned step-3 fighter filter.
UNSURECannot decide from available evidence — abstention beats guessing Fail-open: the article posts normally, no claim invented. Matcher API errors resolve the same way.
The normalizeVerdict gate (added by the first autonomous run, 2026-08-08). Tool schemas are guidance to the model, not a guarantee — Haiku has answered with an off-menu claim type (prediction) and could MATCH a claim id it was never offered. Every verdict is validated before the pipeline trusts it: off-enum type → other; off-enum sourcing → reported (junk can never born-confirm); MATCH on an unoffered id → UNSURE (also closes a foreign-key crash path). Ids compare as strings, because Postgres returns bigints as "7" while the model answers 7 — a naive check would have silently downgraded every match.

Prompt-contract rules learned from live data (each one bought by a real failure in bootstrap testing):

  • Reactions are NEW, not MATCH — "X reacts to Y's prediction" is different news from the prediction.
  • Peripheral mentions are NO_CLAIM, not WRONG_SUBJECT — an early prompt dropped division news that involved the fighter as context; readers wanted it.
  • Canonical text must be strictly evidence-supported (headline + body excerpt) — the model once invented "UFC defeat at the White House." Now: never invent; too ambiguous → UNSURE.
  • Headline-blindness is fixed for new items (2e): a body that clearly concerns the fighter overrides a headline that never names them ("Usman walks through the division"). The pre-2e archive has no bodies, so old items of that shape stay dropped — accepted.
  • Furniture mentions fixed (2026-08-09): the 2e watch item fired — three digest posts whose only "mention" of the fighter was invisible markup (an image alt, a URL slug) or a "LATEST NEWS" promo block. Two-layer fix: matchesFighter now searches reader-visible text only (htmlToText before matching, kills the markup vectors upstream of Gate 1), and the matcher rules gained an explicit line: a fighter appearing nowhere in the shown text, or only in site furniture, is WRONG_SUBJECT. Prose mentions ("Usman's manager targets Fighter C") still post.
  • Digest tier shipped (2026-08-09): not everything the matcher lets through earns a headline. WRONG_SUBJECT drops an article outright; tangential (new, downstream of the matcher) folds it into a shared "↘ Also mentioning: Source1 · Source2" line instead — still posted=true, still linked, just not a bullet of its own. Rule (lib/tier.js): headline never names the fighter, body ≥300 characters (a floor — a 141-char blurb can't support a mention count), and the body names them ≤1×. Threshold measured over the archive: claim-bearing articles name the fighter 2–12× in their body, the junk cluster 0–1×, a clean gap. Claim-producing items are exempt by construction.
  • Duplicated paragraphs were feeding the tier rule a false mention count (2026-08-10): Sports Illustrated renders its own lede paragraphs twice inside <article> (measured live: 24 paragraphs, 17 unique). A story about an untracked fighter that named Fighter C in exactly one passing sentence therefore counted 2 mentions — one over the demote threshold — and posted a full headline in the Fighter C digest it should not have earned. The tier rule itself was never wrong; the body handed to it was. Fixed in lib/extract.js: a paragraph identical to an earlier one is dropped before the mention count runs — both where the extractor reads <article>/<p> tags directly, and at rung 0, where a publisher's own feed ships the whole article (content:encoded) and would otherwise flatten to one line before any duplicate could be spotted. verify-body-dedup.js guards both paths; a walkthrough of this exact item, gate by gate, is linked at the foot of this page.

6The lifecycle: rumor to confirmed

Statuses live on the claim, never on articles, and transitions are driven by rules, not an LLM — the LLM's job ends at "what does this article assert, and how is it sourced?" Phase 1 deliberately ships the conservative core: claims are born rumor (or born confirmed via an official source), and the only path to confirmed is ufc.com asserting it. Counting "two independent sources" waits for phase 2, when article bodies make independence detectable (§7).

Fig. 5 — The state machine. Solid = live today (phase 1). Dashed = designed, waiting on phase 2.
StatusEntry rule (deterministic)
RumorLive New claim from any non-official source, whatever its confidence language — conservative by design
ConfirmedLive ufc.com asserts it (born confirmed, or rumor → confirmed with a threaded ✅ reply). The official-source list is one domain because all three fighters are UFC; pflmma.com parked in config for the day a watched fighter signs elsewhere. Record trackers (Tapology, Sherdog, ESPN) are high-credibility media — they will corroborate in phase 2, never born-confirm.
DeniedPhase 2 A source with stance denies attaches and rules rank it (official denial > original outlet's walk-back > other). Stance is already captured today; the transition isn't wired.
StalePhase 2 No new evidence for ~14 days (window is a guess; MMA negotiation cycles may argue for 30)
SupersededPhase 2 New claim created with supersedes pointing here (date change, opponent change)
The armed trap. Claim #3 — Fighter A's UFC Paris announcement, rumor, five sources — sits waiting for ufc.com coverage. When it arrives, the bot's first autonomous rumor→confirmed threaded reply fires with zero code changes.

7Independence — the hard 20% Phase 2

Designed, not built — but 2e delivered its prerequisite (bodies + real URLs), so this is now buildable. "Two sources" is worthless if the second copied the first: XSPORT translating MMA Junkie is one reporting chain, not two. Corroboration hinges on telling echoes from independent reports. Signals, cheapest first:

  • Attribution text (strongest). Echoes usually confess in their own body: "as reported by MMA Junkie". The matcher reads bodies in 2e anyway — an attributes_to field comes free.
  • Original reporting markers. "told us", "in an interview with this publication", exclusive quotes → origin candidate.
  • Timing. Earliest sighting in a cluster is the presumed origin (verified on live data: MMA Junkie preceded both echoes).
  • Same-org detection. A curated outlet-family map, grown lazily as false corroborations appear.

Default when unsure: echo. The asymmetric cost rules this: a false "confirmed" tells the group fiction is fact; a delayed confirmation just waits for the promotion's own announcement, which always comes. Bias toward skepticism — the same reasoning that made phase 1 ufc.com-only.

8What the group actually sees

Posting is "one message per claim event", not per article. The claim type picks the ceremony; the claim event picks the timing. Headlines post as-is when they're in the group's languages (uk/en); anything else is translated to English by Gemini and labeled "(translated from es)" — one language, never both. The headline is plain text; the source name is the clickable link.

Claim eventPost behavior
New claim, born confirmed, type announcement Live 🚨 Ceremonial standalone post, loud
New claim, rumor, lifecycle type (announcement / negotiation / injury / result) Live 🕵️ Rumor line at the top of the hourly digest — the group loves gossip, it just needs honest labeling
Rumor → confirmed Live ✅ Reply to the original rumor message (via stored tg_message_id) — the threaded story
Everything else claim-worthy or claim-free (quotes, NO_CLAIM, UNSURE) Live One line in the hourly digest, silent delivery
Corroboration attached (still rumor) Live No message — evidence recorded silently. (Display of "also covered by…" is a phase-2 taste decision: edit vs. reply.)
Rumor → denied · superseding claims Phase 2 Reply to original: "❌ Fight is off per …" / "📅 Moved to June 6"

9Operations & reliability

The bot runs unattended; every failure mode found in production has a specific answer:

FailureAnswerPrinciple
Google News 503s (load-shedding of shared cloud IPs — the laptop gets 200s while Cloud Run gets 503s: IP reputation) Cron at :17 (dodges the top-of-hour stampede) + one retry per feed after 75s (waves outlast 30s — measured, tuned) + since 2e, six direct publisher feeds keep delivering even when Google blanks a whole run Measure, then tune — then diversify
A run fails anyway Loses nothing: the 24h fetch window means the next hourly run re-covers it. Data loss starts only after ~24h of continuous outage. Overlapping idempotent sweeps
Repeated failures Cloud Monitoring alert emails on 2+ failed attempts within 2h (single blips don't deserve inbox noise); fatal in-run errors additionally DM the admin directly — never the group Alert on patterns, not blips
Matcher LLM down or confused Fail open: UNSURE → the article still posts via article-level rules, no claim invented; normalizeVerdict catches malformed answers before they hit the database Never lose news; abstention beats invention
Wrong confirmation ever issued Status is derived state — the rules engine can recompute any claim's status from stored evidence at any time; unmerge = delete a claim_sources row and rerun Make wrong states cheap to repair
Runaway cost max-instances=1, request-based billing, Anthropic spend cap; LLM spend tracks the news rate, not the archive size Structural ceilings, not vigilance

Total: still under two dollars a month. 2e added a discovery rail (6 outlet feeds, fetched free) and a body-fetch step (plain HTTP GETs to publisher sites, also free) — neither touches a billed API, so the wider funnel shows up only as more Haiku matcher calls, and even doubling that volume barely moves the total. The escalation-ladder economics hold: the archive can grow 100× and only the (free) pgvector search notices. Fits the planned $10 Anthropic cap with a wide margin. Exact vector scans are fine at this scale; an HNSW index becomes worth it around ~100K+ rows — years away.

11The autonomous improvement loop

The system maintains itself. A scheduled self-improvement session runs every ~6 hours (~18:47 / 00:47 / 06:47 / 12:47 PT — between hunter runs) and:

  • Reads its continuity first: docs/checkin-log.md (the append-only run log — each session's trace, newest on top), TODO.md, and recent git history.
  • Audits the last window: run results, matcher verdicts, held items, claim births, feed health — data first, opinions second.
  • Acts under the decide-act-report policy: when in doubt, take the best decision for the project and report it post-operationally for correction — don't stall. Ask-first is reserved for: destroying schema/data, adding paid services, contradicting a documented decision, or changing what the group experiences in an undesigned way.
  • Commits every session — even a no-change run appends its log entry. Then posts a concise chat report: 📊 data findings · 🔧 changes · 💡 proposals · 👁 watch items.

Hard rules baked into every run: never post to the group or any DM; never print secret values (command substitution only); never delete data. The first autonomous run already shipped real code — the normalizeVerdict gate (§5), including catching the pg-bigint-vs-JSON-number id comparison bug live.

Parked evolutions (specs written, deliberately not built): docs/sandboxed-autonomy.md — moving this loop into an ephemeral sandbox (GitHub Actions cron) with scoped credentials so even a fully poisoned run is harmless; and hourly database snapshots to an always-free GCS bucket (TODO), since Neon's free tier keeps only a 6-hour restore window.

12Roadmap & watch items

Build sequence ahead:

  • 2e — SHIPPED 2026-08-08 (see §4): six direct outlet feeds, wrapped-URL decode, zero-dep body extraction, matcher reads excerpts, full claims re-bootstrap (claims #9–16, drifted links cleaned, argmax mismatches 7→2). Phase 2's prerequisite is delivered.
  • 5 phase 2 — the full lifecycle, NOW UNBLOCKED: independence-counted corroboration (attribution text is readable in bodies), denials, supersede flow, corroboration display (edit vs. reply — taste decision pending).
  • Step 6 — conversational responder with memory and web search ("where to watch?"), through the dormant webhook service. This is where Mastra + TypeScript enter — single structured calls (the matcher) never needed an agent framework; a tool-using conversational agent does.
  • Deploy automation: GitHub remote + Actions (also the prerequisite for the sandboxed autonomy spec and phone-started cloud sessions).

Standing watch items (tracked by the autonomous loop):

  • 2e telemetry: decode success rate (fast path 3/3 at launch), per-outlet fetch 403s, dup-hold volume with two rails on. Which extraction rung wins is now QUERYABLE (body_via, shipped 2026-08-09) instead of console-only — the gap that hid Sherdog's 4/4 ~75-char og-description blurbs from every prior audit.
  • Peripheral body-mention items from outlet feeds: the furniture-mention leak was fixed 2026-08-09 (visible-text gate + WRONG_SUBJECT rule for site furniture); still watch that genuine prose-mention stories keep posting and real news isn't over-dropped.
  • Digest tier (2026-08-09): tangential articles fold into an "also mentioning" line rather than a bullet (§5). Watch the demotion rate against the ~17% measured on the archive — it was measured on a backfilled, not organically-fetched, sample. Cross-rail feed reuse (read a Bloody Elbow article's own feed text instead of a 403ing direct fetch, when Google discovers the same story first) is designed but backlogged — deliberately, until it can be done without hardcoding per-outlet fetch behaviour, since adding athletes may change which outlets/feeds/ extraction strategies matter at all.
  • 503s under the 75s retry; direct feeds now cover a Google blackout, so escalation is only about lost Google-exclusive discoveries.
  • The 0.80 embedding threshold — revisit if false holds appear.
  • Canonical-name flubs (an Islam/Khabib mix-up observed once) — Sonnet escalation if it becomes a pattern.
  • Multi-claim articles (v1 extracts one primary claim), cross-fighter claims ("Fighter B vs Fighter A" would touch two watchlists), storyline threading above claims, a lazy source blocklist for keyword-stuffing aggregators, the 14-vs-30-day staleness window — all observed, parked until the data insists.
